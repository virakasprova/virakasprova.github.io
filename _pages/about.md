I am a Computer Science PhD student at the **University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign**, working on AI safety and trustworthy language models.

My research asks when models agree with users at the expense of being right. Sycophancy is usually studied as one behavior in single-turn chat, but it looks different once a model sits inside a longer interaction — a multi-turn conversation, a debate between agents, or a tool-using agent whose actions have real consequences. I want to know how that structure changes what sycophancy *is*, how it should be measured, and whether defenses built for one setting survive in another.

More broadly, I am interested in agent safety and control. As language models acquire tools, memory, and autonomy, the safety question shifts from what a model says to what it does: when it should decline to act, whether its safeguards survive long-running deployment, and how much of its behavior we can actually govern.
